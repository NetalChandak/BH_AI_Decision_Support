# Methodology — from evidence to 2030 working estimate

## 1. Principle

The analysis uses a **bounded scenario approach** rather than a single deterministic prediction.

### Chain

`Current baseline → policy/target trajectory → NoRA structural advantage → infrastructure delivery → behavioural adoption → 2030 working estimate → sensitivity range`

## 2. Public transport mode share

The working estimate is calibrated between the current Riyadh baseline (~9%) and the NoRA long-term ambition (40%+), while recognising that:

- NoRA is greenfield;
- higher density and TOD can support stronger transit adoption;
- the NoRA mobility strategy is designed around integrated public transport and active mobility;
- transit network delivery is still subject to planning, testing and phasing;
- 2030 is materially earlier than the long-term development horizon.

The result is intentionally expressed as **25–30%** rather than a single precise point forecast.

## 3. Internal sustainable modes

The NoRA framework identifies a 50%+ KPI for internal trips by sustainable modes and also contains benchmarking/target-setting material with a recommended 70–80% range. Because the latter is a long-term ambition rather than an observed 2030 outcome, the working 2030 estimate is set at approximately **45%**, with a sensitivity range of **35–55%**.

## 4. Internalisation

The NoRA framework has a 70%+ long-term KPI for trips internalised within NoRA. The working 2030 estimate is **~55–60%**, with a sensitivity range of **~50–65%**, reflecting a transitional development and mobility network.

## 5. Population

The source indicates a recommended mid-growth population scenario of approximately **3.0M ±7.5%**. The package uses **~2.5–3.5M** as a rounded sensitivity context rather than treating population as a precise 2030 occupancy forecast.

## 6. Why ranges are preferred

The source explicitly indicates that mode-share targets are being tested using mobility network and traffic scenarios and that detailed demand/capacity work remains part of the mobility roadmap. A range is therefore more defensible than false precision.
