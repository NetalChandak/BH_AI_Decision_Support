# NoRA Mobility 2030 — Executive Summary

## Question

What is a plausible 2030 mobility outcome for NoRA when current conditions, project ambitions, development characteristics and uncertainty are considered independently of the RUA/CMP target?

## Working answer

| Metric | 2030 working estimate | Sensitivity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Public transport mode share | **25–30%** | **~19–35%** |
| Internal trips by sustainable modes | **~45%** | **~35–55%** |
| Trips internalised within NoRA | **~55–60%** | **~50–65%** |
| Population context | Phasing-dependent | **~2.5–3.5M** |

## What is evidence vs. model

The NoRA source provides a Riyadh PT baseline of approximately 9%, a 40%+ public transport ambition, a 50%+ KPI for internal sustainable trips, a 70%+ internalisation KPI and a ~3.0M ±7.5% population scenario. It also identifies greenfield development, density, TOD and early integrated transit/accessibility planning as reasons NoRA can aspire to stronger non-car mobility than the wider Riyadh context.

The 2030 values above are **analytical working estimates** produced by applying a transitional-adoption assumption to those source anchors. They are not quoted from the NoRA source.

## Comparison with RUA/CMP

The RUA CMP states a normal-weekday external modal split of 60% public transport / 40% car and an internal split of 54% walking / 30% PRT / 16% micromobility. Those values sum to 100% sustainable internal modes. They are retained as a **comparable planning benchmark**, not as an input to the NoRA forecast.

The comparison suggests that the RUA/CMP planning target assumes substantially stronger public-transport and internal sustainable-mode performance than the independent transitional NoRA 2030 working estimate.

## Main insight

The useful output is not a single magic number. It is a traceable range showing how the result moves when transit delivery, accessibility, TOD effectiveness, parking/TDM and behavioural adoption change.

## Decision use

Use this package to:

- challenge whether a target is plausible by 2030;
- identify the assumptions that matter most;
- identify evidence gaps requiring further study;
- frame the next-stage travel-demand modelling scope;
- create a repeatable AI-assisted forecasting workflow.

## Validation requirement

The working estimate should be replaced or recalibrated when the formal NoRA demand/capacity model and approved mobility plan are available.
